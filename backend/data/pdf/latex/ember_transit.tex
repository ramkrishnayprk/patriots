\section*{Synopsis}
When three wildfires merge outside the mountain city of Vesper, every highway
closes within an hour. Suspended rail engineer Jules Arlen is ordered back to
the control center after the automated evacuation system fails. The only route
left is an unfinished freight loop known as the Ember Line.

Jules boards the final train with conductor Omar Vey and emergency physician
Emi Tan. They must carry eight hundred passengers through smoke, damaged
switches, and a tunnel whose ventilation system has lost power. A remote
dispatcher repeatedly diverts them toward a private terminal, forcing Jules to
decide whether the failures are accidental. The rescue becomes a moving
investigation as the train outruns the fire.

\section*{Themes and Style}
The action is built around mechanical problem solving, teamwork, and the
geography of the rail network. Long takes move through crowded carriages while
exterior sequences emphasize the scale of the fire without turning it into
spectacle. The central conflict concerns public duty, infrastructure neglect,
and who receives priority during a disaster.

\section*{Production Notes}
The train interiors were mounted on hydraulic platforms, and most firelight was
created practically with programmable amber panels. The fictional production
is rated PG-13 for sustained peril, action violence, and smoke-related imagery.
